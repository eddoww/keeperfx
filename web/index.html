How to play

  1. Get Dungeon Keeper. You need an original Dungeon Keeper or Dungeon Keeper Gold; the GOG release works.
  2. Choose its folder. The page reads the 16 files KeeperFX needs from it (and the soundtrack, if it's there) and keeps them in this browser. You only do this once per browser.
  3. Play. The campaign, free play levels, skirmish against the computer, saves and settings all work as in the desktop KeeperFX.

Online play

Host a game

Press Host online game. You get a five-letter lobby code and an invite link at the top of the screen. Send the link to your friends; up to four keepers can play, and others can watch as spectators.

Join a game

Open an invite link, type a code next to Join, or pick an open lobby in the game under Multiplayer → Online. The host chooses the map and the lobby settings (computer players, cheats, spectators) and starts the game.

Accounts

Optional. Guests can host and join. Sign in to play under your own name every time. Your password never leaves your browser: it is turned into a key here, and only a hash of that key is stored.

How it works

This is KeeperFX's own multiplayer: every player runs the full game, and the keepers' orders are exchanged each turn, so all games stay identical. A lobby server on Cloudflare's network relays that traffic, starting close to whoever opens the lobby.

  • Everyone needs their own copy of Dungeon Keeper, picked in their own browser.
  • Online games are between browsers on this site; desktop KeeperFX uses a different connection, so the two can't meet.
  • The game runs at the pace of the slowest player, so a weak computer or connection slows everyone a little. If the host leaves, the others carry on on their own.
  • Keep the tab in front during a game: browsers slow down background tabs.

Controls and tips

Mouse

Click the game to capture the mouse, as the desktop game does; press Esc to release it. Left click to dig, build and pick up creatures; right click to drop them or slap.

Screen

F11 or the Fullscreen button for the whole screen. The game uses your screen's full resolution, sharp on high-DPI displays.

Saves

Saves, settings and replays stay in this browser. Screenshots (Shift+C) are also saved to your downloads.

Questions

Is this an emulator?

No. It is KeeperFX, the open-source remake of the Dungeon Keeper engine, compiled to WebAssembly. Nothing from DOS or Windows runs here.

Why do I need the original game?

KeeperFX is a new engine, but a few of the original's files (palettes and sound banks) are not its own to give away. They stay on your computer: the page reads them from the folder you choose and never uploads them.

How much does it download?

The game and the data for the menus and the first levels come to about 110 MB; more loads as you reach new campaigns and levels. Your browser keeps it, so later visits start quickly.

Which browsers work?

A current browser on a computer, with WebGL 2: Chrome and Edge are tested; Firefox and Safari should work. A mouse and keyboard are needed, so phones and tablets aren't supported.

Something went wrong. How do I report it?

When the game stops, use Download log, or hover the top right corner while playing for the log button, and include it with your report.

How do I start over?

Forget my DK files removes the Dungeon Keeper files and music from this browser; clearing the site's data in your browser also removes saves and settings.
